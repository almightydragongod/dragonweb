Global Mythology

Dragons Across Civilizations

Every great civilization bore witness to the dragon — in fire, in water, in the stars themselves.

CHINA

Chinese Dragon

Lóng 龍 — Celestial Serpent of Heaven

The Lóng is revered as the ultimate symbol of imperial power, cosmic harmony and divine luck. Unlike Western counterparts, Chinese dragons are benevolent, controlling rainfall, rivers and seas. They possess nine anatomical attributes: camel head, deer horns, rabbit eyes, bull ears, snake neck, frog belly, carp scales, eagle claws, and tiger paws.

  • ⚡ Controls lightning, wind and rain
  • 🌊 Guards underwater palaces
  • 👑 Symbol of the Emperor's divine right
  • 🔮 Holds a flaming pearl of wisdom

INDIA

Naga & Vritra

नाग — Cosmic Serpent Dragon

Indian dragon mythology centers on the Naga — half-human, half-cobra beings of immense divine power — and Vritra, the great cosmic serpent-dragon slain by Indra to release the world's waters. Nagas guard treasures and sacred springs; they are beings of tremendous wisdom who sometimes take human form and intermarry with mortals.

  • 💎 Guardians of treasures & secret knowledge
  • 🌿 Connected to water, earth and fertility
  • 🧬 Can shapeshift into human form
  • ☀️ Vritra blocked the cosmic rivers of heaven
Wisdom
Mysticism

ENGLAND

English Dragon

Draco — Fire Drake of the Isles

The English/European dragon is the quintessential apex predator — a four-legged, winged reptilian beast breathing devastating fire. From Beowulf's hoard-guardian to the Red Dragon of Wales, these creatures embody primal chaos, greed and raw destructive power. They are intelligent treasure-hoarders of fearsome cunning.

  • 🔥 Breathes fire hot enough to melt steel
  • 🏰 Sleeps atop mountains of gold treasure
  • 🛡️ Scales harder than plate armour
  • 🧠 Possesses vast, ancient cunning

Dragon Species & Related Entities

Dragon Face

Unicode U+1F409. Added in Emoji 1.0 (2015). Represents the traditional Eastern serpentine dragon with flowing whiskers — culturally defaulting to the Chinese Lóng form across all platforms.

Eastern Origin
🔥

Fire Emoji

Unicode U+1F525. The fire emoji is inseparable from dragon iconography — representing the primal breath weapon. In digital culture, it symbolizes intensity, passion and literal dragon fire.

Universal
🐲

Dragon Head

Unicode U+1F432. The side-profile dragon head emoji — typically depicted in green or jade tones referencing Chinese porcelain dragon art. Used universally across gaming and fantasy contexts.

Cultural Icon
⚡

Thunder Dragon

The Bhutanese flag dragon — Druk — is a thunder dragon. In Dzongkha, Bhutan is called "Druk Yul" (Land of the Thunder Dragon). Lightning is the dragon's secondary weapon after fire.

Himalayan
🐍

Chrysopelea — Flying Snake

The genus Chrysopelea (Paradise Tree Snake) can glide up to 10 meters by flattening its body into a concave shape, creating an aerodynamic wing profile. Scientists believe early dragon myths in Southeast Asia arose from eyewitness accounts of these creatures launching from treetops.

Real Creature
🦎

Draco Volans — Gliding Lizard

The Draco volans is literally named "flying dragon." It extends its elongated ribs to create wing membranes. Found across Southeast Asia, its dramatically coloured wing-flaps likely inspired miniature dragon lore across the region.

Scientific
🌿

Basiliscus — Jesus Christ Lizard

The Basilisk lizard — ancestor of the mythological basilisk — can run across water surfaces. Medieval scholars believed it to be a dragon-kin, a "king of serpents." Its crown-like crest and water-walking ability cemented its legendary status.

Mythic Connection
⚔️

Wyvern

A wyvern is anatomically distinct from a true dragon: it has only two legs (not four), a barbed or club-like tail, and may or may not breathe fire. In heraldry, the wyvern symbolizes strength and valor. It is the true ancestor of the modern fantasy "dragon" in Western culture.

Heraldic
🔱

Worm / Wyrm

The Old English Wyrm (worm) referred to any great serpentine monster. Wyrms are legless, wingless, and immensely large — more like the great cosmic serpents of Norse mythology. They gnaw at the roots of the World Tree.

Norse
🌊

Leviathan

The biblical Leviathan — described in Job 41 as breathing fire, with impenetrable scales, smoke pouring from its nostrils — is textually the closest ancient scripture comes to a full dragon description. Scholars classify it as an oceanic dragon archetype common across Semitic mythology.

Biblical
🧊

Jörmungandr — The World Serpent

Norse mythology's ocean dragon — so vast it encircles the entire Earth and bites its own tail (the Ouroboros). Son of Loki, destined to battle Thor at Ragnarök. The largest dragon-kin in any mythology, its body literally defines the boundary of the world.

Norse Cosmic

The Polymorphic Process

01

Skeletal Compression

Dragon bones — hollow and lightweight for flight — compress via specialised Osteoblastic Hyperregeneration. The 6-limb structure (4 legs + 2 wings) collapses to 4 limbs. Wing metacarpals fold inward and are absorbed into the shoulder blades as vestigial scapular ridges.

02

Neural Rewiring

The dragon's enlarged olfactory and thermal-detection lobes compress. Cranial volume reduces but neural density increases. IQ equivalent paradoxically remains at peak level. The fire-gland nerve cluster enters dormancy, creating the human appendix analogue.

03

Dermal Metamorphosis

Dragon scales — keratin-based armour plates 8cm thick — undergo reverse keratinization. The scale-generating cells enter a latent state beneath human epidermis. Under extreme stress, a polymorphed dragon may involuntarily partially re-scale, revealing their true nature.

04

Telltale Signs

A dragon in human form retains several anomalies: slit-pupil eyes in certain light, body temperature 2°C above human norm, exceptional bone density, slight iridescence of skin in UV light, and an unconscious hoarding behavior of precious metals.

Xenomythology

Mythical Beings of Dragon Blood

Creatures across world mythology who share draconic origins, anatomy or essence.

🔥

Salamander

Medieval alchemy believed the salamander to be an elemental fire-lizard, born from and immune to flame — a proto-dragon of fire incarnate. Paracelsus classified it as a genuine elemental being.

Alchemical Europe
🦅

Garuda

The great Hindu/Buddhist divine eagle — enemy and controller of Nagas (serpent-dragons). A being of solar fire, gold-feathered and immense. Often depicted battling dragon-serpents. A winged divine predator, the celestial counterpart to the earth dragon.

Hindu-Buddhist
🌀

Ouroboros

The serpent-dragon devouring its own tail — the oldest known draconic symbol, found in Egyptian tomb art 1350 BCE. Represents the eternal cycle of creation and destruction, time without end. Jung identified it as the primordial unconscious dragon archetype.

Egypt / Universal
🐍

Quetzalcoatl

The Feathered Serpent of Mesoamerica — a winged, feathered dragon-deity of wind, air and learning. Arguably the most culturally complex dragon being: simultaneously creator deity, civilizing hero, and doomsday omen. Every major Aztec city contained a pyramid temple to this dragon-god.

Aztec / Maya
🌩️

Thunderbird

The colossal storm-bird of North American indigenous peoples — summoning lightning with its wings, rainfall with its beak. Cryptozoologists link fossil evidence of enormous pterosaur-like creatures to Thunderbird sightings. A feathered dragon analog in every functional sense.

Native American
👁️

Basilisk

The King of Serpents — a crested dragon-serpent whose gaze petrifies and whose breath kills all vegetation. From Pliny the Elder to Harry Potter, the basilisk occupies the terrifying intersection of dragon and death — a draconic being of pure lethal malice.

Roman / Medieval
